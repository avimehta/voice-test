    How did Roman concrete last 2,000 years?

    While modern concrete exposed to saltwater deteriorates within decades, ancient Roman marine concrete has survived for thousands of years with little or no wear. The Romans achieved this incredible durability by mixing volcanic ash with quicklime. When seawater seeped into tiny cracks within the submerged concrete, it triggered a surprising chemical transformation. The saltwater reacted with minerals in the volcanic rock to produce rare aluminous tobermorite crystals. These growing crystals reinforced the material and resisted fracturing, making the concrete stronger over time rather than washing it away.

When Romans mixed this ash two-to-one with lime and added water, it triggered a chemical reaction at room temperature. It seems this pozzolanic reaction created strong, insoluble compounds that could harden even underwater, unlike traditional lime mortars.     Why are leap seconds being abolished?

    Farewell to the Leap Second · 1:53

    Transcript

    International timekeepers have decided to officially bring an end to the leap second. In twenty twenty-two, the General Conference on Weights and Measures adopted a resolution to redefine Coordinated Universal Time and abolish these periodic adjustments. Under this plan, leap seconds will be phased out entirely by twenty thirty-five. The change keeps the civil second completely stable and equal to the standard scientific second. This likely means modern clocks will no longer face sudden discontinuities from irregular days.

    To understand why this matters, it helps to look at why leap seconds were created in the first place. Between nineteen seventy-two and twenty sixteen, timekeepers occasionally added these one-second adjustments to Coordinated Universal Time. They were meant to bridge the gap between ultra-precise atomic clocks and observed solar time. While atomic time ticks along steadily, the Earth's rotation is uneven and gradually slowing down. Whenever the two drifted apart, a leap second was inserted at the end of June or December, creating a day of irregular length.

    Without these adjustments, every day on our civil clocks will be precisely eighty-six thousand four hundred seconds long. Coordinated Universal Time will stay at a constant offset of thirty-seven seconds from International Atomic Time. As a result, sundials and the sun's position in the sky will slowly drift out of sync with our clocks. Eventually, in the distant future, a full leap hour might become necessary to catch back up. It seems that until then, our digital world will enjoy steady, uninterrupted time.

    What will the stock market do next year?

    Can We Predict Next Year's Market? · 2:01

    Transcript

    Trying to determine the future value of a company's stock is a tempting puzzle because being right can bring massive profits. However, a leading financial view called the efficient market hypothesis warns that stock prices are inherently unpredictable. This idea states that current share prices already reflect every piece of publicly available information and rational expectations. Because today's price already accounts for a company's past history, it seems unlikely that anyone can reliably tell us where the market will go next year.

    So if today's price already holds everything we know, what actually makes a stock move tomorrow? According to the efficient market hypothesis, prices shift when newly revealed information about a company's prospects arrives. That fresh news is almost immediately absorbed into the current stock price. Beyond company news, prices also move in response to changes in the market generally. Finally, stocks experience random movements around the value that reflects all existing information. Because sudden news and random swings cannot be predicted, what the market will do next year remains essentially anyone's guess.

    Still, not everyone agrees that the market is impossible to read. Many analysts use a method called technical analysis to forecast where prices are headed. They study past market data, focusing primarily on price and trading volume, to spot patterns. Supporters of this approach use myriad methods and technologies to try to gain future price information. However, the efficient market hypothesis disputes whether technical analysis actually works. Research on whether technical analysis offers any real benefit has produced mixed results, which leaves the direction of next year's market an open question.
